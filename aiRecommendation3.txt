I went through the notebook structure you uploaded. It confirms something important.

This is **not** a project that is "missing a better CNN." You've already explored several meaningful variants:

* **R1**: R-guided model (Cross-Entropy)
* **R2**: R-guided + weighted Focal Loss
* **R3**: Standard Focal Loss
* **R4**: Physiology-aware decoder with a frozen R1 backbone
* LUDB held out completely for external testing

So you're already beyond the stage where "try another loss function" is likely to produce a breakthrough.

---

## I think your biggest bottleneck is this

The baseline is trained and evaluated under one distribution.

Your R-guided model is evaluated on **another distribution (LUDB)**.

That means you're no longer fighting optimization.

You're fighting **domain shift**.

The evidence supporting this is:

* P improves reasonably.
* T remains the weak point.
* Multiple losses don't fix it.
* Physiological decoder doesn't suddenly solve it.

Those are classic symptoms of a representation mismatch rather than an optimization problem.

I would stop trying new architectures.

Instead I'd try to extract the **maximum gain per hour**.

# The idea I actually like most

This wasn't in our earlier discussions.

Since you know exactly where R is...

why make the network search the entire beat?

Suppose statistics say

```
95% of T onsets

occur

180–420 ms
```

after R.

During decoding

mask everything outside

```
[R+60, R+170]
```

samples.

Not hard labels.

Just

```
Probability outside region

×

0.1
```

instead of

1.0.

Now the decoder can't hallucinate a T wave before the QRS.

That's a much stronger physiological prior than simply centering the beat.

It also remains interpretable.

## One final thought

Given your application timeline, I would also start thinking strategically about **what makes the strongest paper**, not just the strongest model.

Right now, you already have a compelling result:

> A physiologically guided reformulation improves external generalization compared with optimization-based improvements alone.

Even if you only gain another **2–3%** on LUDB, the paper becomes much stronger if you can also explain **why** those remaining failures occur. Reviewers generally value that level of analysis more than another half-point of F1 without insight.

So my next question wouldn't actually be *"How do we increase T-wave F1?"* It would be:

> **"What exactly are the T waves that the model still misses?"**

Once we answer that, I think we'll know whether the right fix is domain adaptation, a better temporal prior, or a small architectural change—instead of having to guess.
